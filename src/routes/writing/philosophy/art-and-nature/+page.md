It's obvious to most people that the delineation of all things human made as
"not nature" and all things bereft of mans influence as "natural" is imprecise;
If the beehive is natural, then why not the office building? This delineation
then is more practical it's application than representative of some clearly
articulable fundamental truth.

In my estimation, this delineation is formalizing all things into two
fundamental categories:

- Art
- Nature

Nature is, following from it's typical colloquial usage, all things bereft of
intentional human intervention. Art then is all things that are not Nature -
that is all things that are or originated of direct and intentional human effort
and intervention.

This seemingly generates contradictions and questions and fuzzy edges. For
example: The river dam that inadvertently starves a species of salamander of
their necessary and natural habitat, yielding rapid population decline. Is that
genocide art or nature? For surely the consequences were wholly, or at least
primarily, derivative of the human action in building the dam. Classifying the
resulting amphibicide as art then may seem intuitive. Here instead, we must
understand the criticality of intention in our classification. The dam was art
to be sure, however excellently or blundurously executed. The unintentional
consequences of that act was a direct and lethal impact on the surrounding
nature, and was nature. Now one may say the dam was a crude art if it's
ramifications weren't adequately considered, as in this hypothetical they
clearly weren't, but they _must_ say it was art nonetheless.

Now, in the other case, where the architects, and builders, and environmental
surveyors, and regulators did consider the natural ramifications, and chose to
execute due to or in spite of them, then the natural genocide of the salamanders
instead becomes an artful genocide, distinct and connected to the art damming.

Lastly, and to be clear, if the effected downstream population is not that of
salamanders, but rather of humans, nothing changes.
